Where magic fits in a wedding day

The best slot is the cocktail hour. That's when the wedding party is off at photos and guests are standing around with a drink. Tripp moves group to group performing card magic in people's hands, so nobody notices the wait.

Other couples book magic between dinner and the first dance, or as a short featured set during the reception. Because close-up magic needs no stage or sound, it fits any venue, from a downtown hall to a farmyard tent.

Why close-up magic works at weddings

  • It mixes the two families. Strangers react to the same moment and start talking.
  • It works for every age. Grandparents, kids and the groomsmen all get pulled in.
  • It's portable. Tripp performs standing, in small groups, indoors or outdoors.
  • It creates moments for your photographer. Guest reactions make great candid shots.

What's included

  • Strolling close-up magic for 60 to 120 minutes
  • A planning call to match timing to your schedule
  • Optional moment for the couple, such as a signed-card reveal
  • Travel within the Regina area, with farther venues quoted on request

Planning checklist for couples

  • Pick the slot first. Cocktail hour is the most common; tell Tripp your photo schedule so he knows how long guests will be waiting.
  • Loop in your planner or MC. They'll know when Tripp starts and where guests will be gathered.
  • Think about the space. Standing groups, lounge seating or tables all work. Outdoor ceremonies are fine too; just mention wind or shade.
  • Tell your photographer. Guest reactions to close-up magic make some of the best candid shots of the day.
  • Plan a moment for yourselves. Ask about a signed-card reveal or a short piece performed just for the two of you.

Have a large guest list? Strolling magic scales well, because Tripp works through the room group by group. Give him your headcount and he'll recommend how long to book.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When should the magician perform at a wedding?

Most couples book the cocktail hour, while they're away for photos. Between dinner and the dance is another popular slot.

Does a wedding magician need a stage or microphone?

No. Strolling close-up magic needs no stage, sound or setup.

Is magic good for a mixed-age wedding crowd?

Yes. Close-up magic plays to kids, grandparents and everyone in between.

How far in advance should we book?

As soon as your date is set. Summer Saturdays are usually the first to go.
